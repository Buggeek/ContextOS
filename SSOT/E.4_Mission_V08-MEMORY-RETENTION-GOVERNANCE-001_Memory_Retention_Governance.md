## Canonical Model

Retention is a policy axis independent from:

- epistemic support;
- governance lifecycle;
- strategic belief;
- sensitivity;
- access;
- retrieval relevance;
- Activation relevance;
- temporal validity and supersession.

Minimum retention states:

```text
active
historical
archived
operationally_forgotten
content_removed
unknown
```

These states are not a mandatory linear lifecycle. `restricted` is an access
result. `expired` is a review condition. `superseded` is temporal context.
None independently authorizes a transition.

---

## Policy Resolution

The deterministic resolution model:

1. collects all applicable policy and obligation sources;
2. validates identity, version, scope, owner, and authority;
3. applies Doctrine and safety as non-relaxable bounds;
4. treats legal, regulatory, contractual, audit, investigation, safety, and
   data-subject duties as simultaneous constraints;
5. applies organization, operation/project, memory-form, and item inheritance;
6. permits only bounded, authorized, time-limited exceptions;
7. uses the most restrictive access/mutation result for compatible rules and
   accumulates preservation duties;
8. blocks incompatible preservation and deletion/minimization duties for
   accountable human resolution;
9. records the full resolution path and unknowns.

There is no fabricated universal legal precedence and no universal retention
duration.

## Authority Boundary

- L1 may inspect policy and propose classification or transition candidates.
- L2 may draft policy and transition decisions.
- L3 explicit human decisions approve policy and exact non-destructive
  transitions.
- Legal/compliance holds require the designated human authority.
- Redaction, minimization, and content removal require separate contracts,
  explicit destructive authority, preflight, confirmation, validation, and
  evidence.
- No L4/L5 destructive retention authority is granted.

Retention authority cannot mutate canonical truth. Context Construction
authority cannot delete memory.

## Deliberate Forgetting Decision

Deliberate forgetting means reducing ordinary organizational use under
governance. It may later be realized through active-memory removal, retrieval
restriction, archival, minimization, expiration review, operational
forgetting, or content removal. These mechanisms remain distinct.

Operational forgetting may preserve content while excluding it from ordinary
Retrieval and Activation. Physical deletion may be irreversible and cannot be
described as recoverable merely because an independent backup might exist.

A permitted tombstone preserves only non-sensitive lineage allowed by the
resolved policy. Deleted lineage is not assumed mandatory when retaining it
would violate the governing duty.

---

## Personal And Organizational Boundary

The primitives generalize to future personal context, but authority does not.
Personal memory introduces individual control, consent, portability,
private/shared boundaries, and data-subject rights. Organizational memory
introduces role authority, contracts, shared accountability, affected
stakeholders, and institutional duties.

Conflicts are surfaced, not resolved through a universal priority rule. This
Mission does not create a personal-memory product.

## Learning

Retention is not primarily a storage optimization. It is a governed decision
about ordinary use, preservation duties, access, affected parties, and
accountability over time. The smallest universal model is therefore a set of
independent axes plus deterministic conflict blocking, not one lifecycle or a
table of default durations.

The next useful Runtime step is read-only resolution. Execution would be
premature because Context OS cannot yet prove organization-specific policy
values, hold authority, or destructive recovery behavior.
