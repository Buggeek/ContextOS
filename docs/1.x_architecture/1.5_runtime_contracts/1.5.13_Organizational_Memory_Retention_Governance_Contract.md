# 1.5.13 - Organizational Memory Retention Governance Contract

## Purpose

This contract defines how Context OS governs preservation, access, archival,
restriction, operational forgetting, and possible future content removal for
Organizational Memory.

It defines decision-complete policy semantics and the implemented read-only
Retention Resolution boundary. It does not execute deletion, archival
movement, expiration, redaction, forgetting, or any retention transition.

Machine representations:

```text
contextos.memory.retention_policy/1
contextos.memory.retention_decision/1
contextos.memory.retention_resolution/1
contextos.memory.retention_resolution_check/1
```

`contextos.memory.retention_decision/1` is a memory-specific profile of the
existing `contextos.decision/1` model, not a parallel decision system.

---

## Product Question

The policy answers:

> Why is this memory preserved, who may access or change its retention state,
> which obligations constrain it, and under what governed conditions may it
> become historical, archived, restricted, operationally forgotten, or a
> candidate for content removal?

Context OS provides universal governance semantics. Each organization remains
accountable for its lawful periods, classifications, duties, and decisions.

---

## Canonical Boundaries

```text
Retention state != truth state
Retention state != authority
Retention state != strategic belief
Sensitivity != retention state
Access != retrieval relevance
Expiration != deletion
Operational forgetting != physical deletion
```

The canonical truth axes remain independent:

| Axis | States |
|---|---|
| Epistemic support | `observed`, `declared`, `inferred`, `derived`, `unknown` |
| Governance lifecycle | `suggested`, `draft`, `reviewed`, `approved`, `canonical` |
| Strategic belief | `hypothesis`, `verified`, `deprecated` |

A canonical item may be archived. A deprecated item may remain preserved. A
rejected proposal may remain decision evidence. A retrieved item may remain
unauthorized. None of these combinations is contradictory.

---

## Minimum Policy Dimensions

Every governed policy or item-level decision must represent, explicitly or as
an inherited value:

| Dimension | Required meaning |
|---|---|
| Identity and scope | Policy id/version, organization, operation/project, memory form, and selected items |
| Purpose | Why preservation or restriction serves continuity, accountability, safety, learning, or an applicable duty |
| Ownership | Accountable Memory Owner and policy owner |
| Memory form and tier | Mission, Decision, Evidence, Outcome, Learning, Context-state transition, or Inbox; working, project, or organizational tier |
| Sensitivity | Organization-defined classification plus handling baseline |
| Affected parties | Data subject, stakeholder, customer, employee, counterparty, or unknown where applicable |
| Access | Who may inspect content, metadata, and lineage |
| Retention state | Current preservation and default-use state |
| Temporal basis | Created, effective, superseded, reviewed, review-due, and condition dates where known |
| Obligations and holds | Legal, regulatory, contractual, audit, investigation, safety, or preservation constraints |
| Retrieval and Activation eligibility | Normal, elevated-authority, excluded, or prohibited use |
| Recovery and irreversibility | Reversible transition, recovery source, or explicit irreversible boundary |
| Lineage | Source identity/hash, prior state, decision, supersession, and permitted tombstone |
| Authority and evidence | Required human roles, approval scope, rationale, and validation evidence |
| Exceptions | Explicitly authorized, time-bounded narrowing or override within higher-order bounds |

No universal duration is required by this contract. An absent organization-
specific duration or obligation remains `unknown` or `decision_needed`.

`Memory Owner` means the existing accountable owner for the governed artifact,
data, project, or organizational scope. It does not introduce a new universal
role.

---

## Retention State Axis

The minimum retention states are:

| State | Meaning | Default retrieval | Reversible? |
|---|---|---|---|
| `active` | Preserved and eligible for routine governed use | policy and access dependent | yes |
| `historical` | Preserved as prior context but not current operating truth | bounded historical retrieval | yes |
| `archived` | Preserved outside normal active retrieval | elevated or explicit historical request | normally yes |
| `operationally_forgotten` | Preserved only where policy requires, but excluded from routine retrieval and Activation | excluded by default | only if content remains and authority allows |
| `content_removed` | Governed content is unavailable; only permitted non-sensitive lineage may remain | prohibited | no promised recovery |
| `unknown` | Retention state cannot be established | excluded pending resolution | n/a |

These states are not a mandatory linear lifecycle. A governed decision may
move an item between compatible states, but no transition is implicit.

`restricted` is not a retention state. It is an access result. `expired` is
not a retention state. It is a condition that may trigger review.
`superseded` is temporal/context status, not permission to archive or delete.

## Review And Candidate Conditions

The policy may identify non-executing conditions:

```text
review_due
expiration_candidate
archival_candidate
forgetting_candidate
minimization_candidate
content_removal_candidate
blocked_by_hold
blocked_by_conflict
decision_needed
```

Candidate status never changes content, access, or retention state. It creates
a requirement for governed review.

---

## Sensitivity And Access

Context OS defines a small interoperable handling baseline:

```text
public | internal | confidential | restricted | unknown
```

Organizations may define more specific classifications but must map them to a
baseline handling level. A lower policy layer may narrow access but may not
relax a higher applicable handling requirement.

Safe default for `unknown` sensitivity:

- preserve the existence and classification gap where permitted;
- exclude content from Activation;
- require elevated human authority for retrieval;
- do not infer that the content is public, deletable, or permanently retained.

Explainable exclusion may reveal item identity, policy id, and reason only
when that metadata is itself permitted. Restricted content must not leak
through titles, snippets, matched terms, hashes, paths, or error messages.

---

## Retrieval And Activation Eligibility

Policy resolution produces separate eligibility results:

```text
normal | elevated_authority | excluded | prohibited | unknown
```

| Retention state | Memory Retrieval baseline | Context Activation baseline |
|---|---|---|
| `active` | normal subject to purpose, access, and relevance | eligible subject to current authority and Minimum Sufficient Context |
| `historical` | bounded historical retrieval | excluded unless the Mission explicitly requires historical context |
| `archived` | elevated-authority or explicit archival request | excluded by default |
| `operationally_forgotten` | excluded | excluded |
| `content_removed` | prohibited; lineage only where permitted | prohibited |
| `unknown` | elevated-authority or excluded | excluded |

Current canonical Context remains authoritative. Retention eligibility cannot
promote historical memory, resolve semantic conflict, or make a retrieved item
applicable.

Saved Continuity reports, Retrieval results, Activation Packages, and
Handoffs invalidate when a material policy version, sensitivity, access,
hold, retention state, retrieval eligibility, Activation eligibility, or
lineage permission changes.

---

## Policy Resolution

Resolution is deterministic but does not invent a universal ranking among
laws, regulations, contracts, or jurisdictions.

1. Collect all policies and obligations applicable to the item, actor,
   purpose, operation, jurisdiction, and time.
2. Verify policy identity, version, scope, owner, and authority.
3. Apply Doctrine and Context OS safety constraints as non-relaxable bounds.
4. Treat legal, regulatory, contractual, audit, investigation, safety, and
   data-subject duties as simultaneous constraints.
5. Apply organization, operation/project, memory-form, and item policies by
   inheritance. Lower scopes may narrow behavior but do not silently relax
   higher constraints.
6. Apply a Mission exception only when the policy permits it and an authorized
   human decision binds exact scope and expiry.
7. For compatible constraints, the most restrictive access and mutation
   result governs. Preservation requirements accumulate.
8. If preservation and deletion/minimization duties conflict, resolution is
   `blocked_by_conflict`; route to accountable legal/compliance and Governance
   humans. Context OS does not choose a law or legal interpretation.
9. Record the complete resolution path and unresolved inputs.

Absence of policy resolves to default deny for mutation and conservative
access. It never resolves to permanent preservation or deletion.

---

## Holds And Conflicting Duties

A hold is an explicit preservation constraint with:

- hold identity and type;
- issuing human authority;
- exact scope;
- rationale and evidence reference;
- effective time;
- review/release conditions;
- sensitivity of the hold itself.

An active hold blocks archival that would impair access, minimization,
operational forgetting, redaction, and content removal unless an authorized
conflict decision explicitly permits a compatible action.

Only the authority designated by the applicable policy may release a hold.
An agent may surface or draft a hold request but may not place, interpret,
override, or release a legal/compliance hold autonomously.

---

## Deliberate Forgetting

Deliberate forgetting is a governed reduction in ordinary organizational use,
not a synonym for deletion.

| Mechanism | Meaning | Content mutation in this contract? |
|---|---|---|
| Active-memory removal | Transition from routine use to historical state | no |
| Retrieval restriction | Preserve content under narrower access | no |
| Archival | Preserve outside normal active retrieval | no |
| Redaction/minimization | Produce a governed reduced representation and constrain the source | prohibited here |
| Expiration candidate | Trigger review after a condition | no |
| Operational forgetting | Exclude from routine Retrieval and Activation while preserving only what policy requires | no |
| Physical deletion/content removal | Irreversible destructive removal unless independent recovery is proven | prohibited here |
| Tombstone/lineage preservation | Retain minimal permitted evidence of identity, transition, authority, and reason | no content deletion here |

No mechanism may be executed silently or solely because a model recommends
it. Redaction, minimization, and content removal require separate contracts,
exact human authority, preflight, confirmation, validation, and evidence.

---

## Memory-Form Baselines

These are safe policy questions, not universal retention periods:

| Memory form | Minimum preservation concern | Default-use concern |
|---|---|---|
| Mission | Preserve governed outcome, authority, decision, and evidence lineage; raw working notes require separate policy | Closed Missions are historical, not active instructions |
| Decision | Preserve accountable rationale, authority, context version, and reversal lineage where permitted | Superseded decisions remain historical and never regain authority through retrieval |
| Evidence | Preserve enough to support the governed claim or decision, subject to sensitivity and applicable duties | Evidence access may be narrower than the decision it supports |
| Outcome | Preserve what was objectively observed and its limitations | Outcome does not prove causal usefulness |
| Learning | Preserve scope, support, uncertainty, and source Mission | Learning remains non-canonical until governed construction/promotion |
| Context-state transition | Preserve previous/new identities, hashes, time, and decision lineage | Historical state is not current canonical Context |
| Evolution Inbox | Preserve unresolved, rejected, deferred, accepted, and superseded disposition lineage | Only unresolved/accepted items are candidates for active consideration |

## Authority Model

| Action | Minimum authority | Human accountability |
|---|---|---|
| Read permitted policy metadata | L0/L1 read scope | Policy owner |
| Propose classification, review, archival, restriction, or forgetting candidate | L1 | Memory Owner reviews |
| Draft policy or transition decision | L2 draft surface | Governance reviewer |
| Approve organization/domain retention policy | L3 explicit decision | Governance Role plus accountable policy owner |
| Approve item transition among non-destructive states | L3 exact scope | Memory Owner; Governance where policy requires |
| Place or release legal/compliance hold | Human-only designated role | Legal/compliance authority of record |
| Execute future non-destructive state transition | L3 exact decision and preflight | Human approver and auditor |
| Redact, minimize, or remove content | Human-only explicit destructive authority; no autonomous agent default | Content owner plus Governance and required legal/compliance role |
| Change canonical truth | Existing Context Construction authority | Separate from retention decision |

New retention capabilities begin at L1. No L4/L5 destructive retention is
authorized by this contract.

### Implemented read-only resolution profile

`RetentionResolutionEngine` consumes one exact memory metadata object plus an
explicit list of versioned policies. The memory input requires `id`, `form`,
`sensitivity`, and `retention_state`; it may also bind organization, operation,
tier, owner, temporal state, truth axes, required policy references, evidence,
metadata visibility, and repository-relative provenance with an expected hash.

The `contextos.memory.retention_resolution/1` derived report records:

- deterministic input, source, memory, and policy fingerprints;
- applicable and non-applicable policies plus unresolved applicability;
- independent results for access, Retrieval, Activation, retention transition,
  and destructive action;
- accumulated preservation requirements, active holds, conflicts, unknowns,
  required human roles, and metadata-safe evidence;
- an explicit read-only/no-authority/no-mutation boundary and invalidation
  conditions.

`contextos.memory.retention_resolution_check/1` re-evaluates the exact current
inputs. Memory metadata, policy version/content, source evidence, consumer,
roles, operations, or evaluation-time drift invalidates the saved resolution.
The check does not refresh policy silently.

No CLI surface is required for this first runtime capability. The public Python
API is the reusable integration surface for Memory Retrieval and Activation.

## Future Transition Validation And Evidence

Before any future transition, validation must prove:

- exact item identity and current content hash;
- exact policy versions and resolution path;
- ownership, sensitivity, access, subject, and jurisdiction fields are known
  or explicitly blocked;
- required authority is current and scoped;
- holds and conflicting duties were checked;
- no truth, authority, or canonical lifecycle transition is implied;
- retrieval and Activation invalidation impact is frozen;
- reversibility or irreversibility is explicit;
- required lineage/tombstone content is permitted and sufficient.

After any future transition, evidence must record:

- before and after state;
- exact affected identities and hashes;
- action scope and actor;
- approval, confirmation, and policy resolution;
- validation result;
- retrieval/package/handoff invalidations;
- recovery result or irreversibility statement;
- lineage/tombstone result without leaking restricted content;
- accountability reference.

Failed validation can never be represented as successful completion.

---

## Recovery And Irreversibility

- `active`, `historical`, `archived`, and `operationally_forgotten` transitions
  may be reversible only while preserved content, policy, access, and evidence
  make restoration possible.
- Restriction reversal requires current authority and a new policy resolution.
- Redaction/minimization may be irreversible for removed fields.
- `content_removed` carries no recovery promise. A backup is a separate
  governed source, not evidence that deletion is reversible.
- Recovery must not bypass a current hold, sensitivity rule, or authority
  boundary.
- Lineage may remain only to the extent policy permits; a tombstone must never
  preserve content that the governing decision prohibited.

---

## Personal And Organizational Memory

The same retention primitives may serve future personal context, but authority
is not interchangeable.

Organizational memory is governed by organizational ownership, roles,
contractual duties, affected stakeholders, and shared accountability.
Personal memory requires explicit individual control, consent, portability,
private/shared boundaries, and applicable data-subject rights.

When personal and organizational duties overlap, Context OS records the
conflict and governing participants. It does not assume organizational control
always overrides the individual or that individual deletion always overrides
a lawful preservation duty.

This contract does not create a personal-memory product.

---

## Prohibited Behavior

This contract does not authorize:

- deletion, redaction, minimization, archival movement, expiration execution,
  automatic forgetting, or storage-tier movement;
- universal retention periods or legal conclusions;
- silent policy inheritance or conflict resolution;
- unrestricted metadata leakage for restricted items;
- changing truth, strategic belief, governance lifecycle, or canonical status;
- autonomous holds, destructive decisions, or destructive execution;
- retention schedulers, backups, external memory, GraphRAG, embeddings,
  Knowledge Engine expansion, Context Graph runtime, or agents.

---

## Acceptance Criteria

- Retention is independent from the three truth axes.
- Active, historical, archived, operationally forgotten, removed, and unknown
  states are distinguishable.
- Sensitivity, access, holds, Retrieval, and Activation eligibility remain
  separate policy results.
- Policy resolution is deterministic where constraints are compatible and
  blocks conflicts requiring human/legal interpretation.
- All foundational memory forms have an explicit policy baseline.
- No duration, legal conclusion, or destructive operation is invented.
- Future destructive behavior requires a separate contract and explicit human
  authority.
- Current Continuity, Retrieval, Activation, Health, and Construction behavior
  remains unchanged.
- Read-only policy resolution is deterministic, metadata-safe, source-bound,
  independently checkable, and grants no authority.
